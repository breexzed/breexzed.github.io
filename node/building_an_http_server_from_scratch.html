Building An HTTP Server From Scratch

documentation -> implementation -> capability

I attempted building an HTTP server once before, as part of the Backend from First Principles: Complete Series track. I got stuck shortly after establishing a TCP connection and sending requests to it — hardcoded request, hardcoded response. Those early stages live in that project's git commit logs.

The first attempt lives in http-from-scratch on GitHub.

Later I came across the insights from On Learning, Finding Projects, and Building Towards a Mind of Gold, and I have a much clearer picture now of how I want to approach building this. I'm still unclear on a lot of the implementation details, but I plan to use this exact medium — this log — to break through them. I know I can do it. I will overcome every block.

Project Overview

I read an intern's article on rate limiting at incident.io, as part of the protocols from On Learning, Finding Projects, and Building Towards a Mind of Gold. Here's the blog: Rate limiting for beginners.

I took it to ChatGPT and used some of the prompts from the protocol I'd followed. The infographic above is the result of that session.

The why, what, and how (task, tool, scope)

I'd already been attempting to build an HTTP server. Then I found the rate-limiting blog, and I wanted to build one at a scale I could actually manage. So I decided — why not use the HTTP server as the base and scale toward the rate limiter, since they sit in the same field? ChatGPT helped me concretize that instinct and gave me the roadmap (and the infographic above) to build:

A small HTTP server in Go that starts as a bare process accepting requests, and gradually evolves into a measurable, resilient, concurrent, performance-tested piece of infrastructure.

Repo: httpd-go

Trapped within constraints that suffocate being and becoming, i untie myself from the shackles bound on me, through the dispersion of my soul and the traces i leave of having existed, homage to the one in me who knows nothing, but wants to learn.

Having seen how i saw myself, i realized, there was little i knew apart from myself. Self-knowledge is no mystery, understanding one thing is an implicit attribution to the one who understands. How does one do it? You know what you want and you want to become this specific type of person. Not accidentally, not curated. Enacted. Being-as-configuration.

My little philosophy on this:

The work of becoming great perhaps is in the act of becoming less. Recognizing the daily struggles of creating order and being one with it within the flux of life's moment.

Do you have a role model or is this dream so unique? What materials do you need to interact with to put yourself into the orbit of the class of such objects? How do you backtrack from where you’ve found what’s closest to your own truth?

Curiosity already brought you here. It will not take you back, only keep you in check. Sustain you in orbit. But the actual work of moving in alignment with the systems of the space you inhabit is solely up to how you can navigate the antigravity of your position.

In relation to the pulling forces of the bodies around you. Distractions, and sometimes, necessities. Calling them for what they are, hindrances to being who you think you could be without hindrances. But being honest with yourself, are you the type of person yet?

If you cannot manage your current life well enough to change its trajectory, do you expect something or someone else to impose order on your behalf for you? How close are you to your own truth in the first place, or is it only just an image in your head?

Seed of becoming planted in self.
